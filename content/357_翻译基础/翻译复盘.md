## 10 月 MTI 测试诗经

中国是诗的国度，而《诗经》是中国诗歌的源头。《诗经》三百零五篇，分《风》、《雅》、《颂》三部分。它们内容丰富，其中有先祖创业的颂歌，祭祀神鬼的乐章；也有贵族之间的宴饮交往，劳逸不均的怨愤；更有反映劳动、打猎、以及大量恋爱、婚姻、社会习俗方面的动人篇章。

> China is well known for its poetry, with ~~The Poetry~~ The Book of Songs as the earliest work in the history of poetry. With 305 poems altogether, The Poetry is divided into three parts, including ~~Feng, Ya and Song~~ Feng (Ballads), Ya (Court Hymn) and Song (Eulogy). Rich in ~~contents~~ content, they include ~~extols~~ eulogies in appreciation of the achievements achieved by ~~people’s ancestors~~, ~~music works~~ ~~themed on~~ worshiping divinities and ~~ghosts~~, and ~~also have~~ works that describe the banquets of the aristocracy and ~~resent on the imbalance between work and spare time~~. Moreover, there are also vivid ~~chapters~~ ballads ~~in terms of~~ depicting labor, hunting, love, marriage and social customs.

> China is a land of poetry, and the very source of its poetic tradition is The Book of Songs, the oldest Chinese <u>anthology</u> that collects 305 poems dating back to the Zhou Dynasty (1046-256 BC). They are divided into three sections: Feng (folk songs), Ya (court hymns) and Song (eulogies), and deal with a wide array of content, from odes to <u>the founding fathers’ extraordinary achievements</u> to <u>music used in worship rituals for deities and spirits</u>, from records of banquets among the aristocracy to <u>expressions of resentment towards harsh labor imposed on the lower class</u>. In addition, there are poems on labor and hunting, along with many engaging pieces that <u>speak of</u> courtship, marriage and social <u>mores</u>.

诗人立足现实而又执著不懈的精神，与儒家所提倡的积极进取的伦理风范相吻合，《诗经》因此被儒家奉为经典，影响了后世士大夫的诗歌观念。诗歌在很大程度上成为中国传统文人抒泄情感的一种主要途径，尤其是在自己的理想受到现实政治的阻碍时，文人就很自然地通过诗歌创作、通过抒情言志的方式宣泄愤懑。这就是孔子所谓“《诗》可以兴，可以观，可以群，可以怨”。

"兴"指通过诗歌激发联想与情感，"观"指观察社会风俗与政治得失，"群"指促进群体交流与和睦，"怨"指表达对时政的讽谏与批判。

> As the poets’ ~~philosophy of relying on realities and perseverance~~ was in line with the positive ethical codes advocated by ~~the~~ Confucianism, ~~The Poetry~~ was designated by ~~the~~ Confucianism as a classic, which created an impact on later officials’ notions of poetry. To a great extent, poetry was a major way for ancient intellectuals ~~of China~~ to express their feelings. In particular, when they encountered political obstacles in the course of pursuing their aspirations, they would naturally vent their indignant feelings ~~through~~ writing poems in which ~~the intellectuals~~ they described their feelings and aspirations. This is what Confucius said, “ ~~The Poetry can be appreciated, seen as a way of making friends and commented.~~” Poetry may serve to inspire, to reflect, to communicate and to complain.

> The authors’ spirit of realism and persistence resonated with the Confucian ethical norms of self-improvement and enterprise. Therefore, the book was enshrined as a Confucian classic and exerted a profound influence on how scholar-officials in later generations perceived poetry. In ancient China, poetry served as scholars’ primary medium of expressing their emotions, and they naturally <u>turned to</u> poems <u>as</u> an outlet for their resentment and aspirations, particularly when their ideals were held back by <u>real politics</u>. As Confucius said, “<u>The Book of Songs can inspire the mind, offer insight, foster social connection, and give voice to discontent.”</u>

这一观点，使得中国传统文人在本质上都成为诗人，使得诗歌成为文人生活中不可缺少的一部分，使得诗歌成为一种个人抒情的手段，从而极大地促进了诗歌的发展，也引导了诗歌对现实人生的深刻关注。《诗经》的流传不衰表明，诗歌从来都不只是聚散悲欢的个人抒写，同时也是时代风貌的深度展示，甚至是时代进步的重要推力。

> This view ensured that traditional Chinese intellectuals became poets in essence, the poetry became an indispensable part of their lives and a means to express ~~one’s~~ their feelings, thus greatly promoting the development of poetry and ~~arousing poets’ attention to~~ real life. The consistent popularity of ~~The Poetry~~ indicates that poetry is not only a description of ~~individuals themselves all long~~, but also an in-depth show of ~~conditions in an era~~ and even an important driver of progress in an era.

> Inspired by this perspective, virtually all traditional Chinese scholars turned into poets, and poetry emerged as an indispensable part of their lives and a means to convey personal emotions. This tradition also boosted the development of poetry and <u>directed its focus to the realities of life</u>. In fact, the enduring popularity of the Book of Songs indicates that poetry is never <u>simply a record of personal stories and emotions</u>; it is a window into the ethos of the times and even a vital driver of its progress.

《诗经》关注现实的热情、强烈的政治和道德意识、真诚积极的人生态度，为后人所继承和发扬，被概括为“风雅”精神。后世文人常在理论和实际中自觉抵制个人趣味，抵制诗歌中的形式主义倾向。自唐以后，历朝历代都有以“风雅”为旨趣的诗歌革新运动，并产生较大的社会影响。以上种种原因，使得“风雅”在传统文化里成为评价诗歌的最高标准。

> ~~The Poetry~~ paid attention to the realistic enthusiasm, strong political and moral sense, and the sincere and positive attitude towards life, which were ~~passed on and~~ developed by later generations and were generalized into the philosophy of “fengya”. In both theory and reality, later intellectuals often opposed individual interests ~~in both theory and reality~~, and the tendency of formalism in poetry. Since the Tang Dynasty, ~~there has been reformatory movements against poetry~~ in every dynasty, which aimed to foster “fengya” and exerted great influence on society. For all these reasons, ~~“fengya”~~ it was seen as the highest standard to appraise a poem in the traditional culture.

> Ardent attention to reality, strong political and moral awareness, and sincere and positive attitudes towards life - these qualities embodied in The Book of Songs were enriched and refined over the following centuries into the spirt of elegance. In both theory and practice, <u>scholars of subsequent generations</u> <u>consciously</u> avoided <u>personal preferences</u> and resisted formalism in poetic creation. From the Tang Dynasty <u>onward</u>, movements aimed at cultivating this aesthetic ideal in poetry arose in every dynasty, generating tremendous social impact. For these reasons, elegance turned into the highest standard for evaluating poetry in traditional Chinese culture.

> Traditional Chinese people's vision of language seemed to overlook the sounds and forms of the chinese language to some extent for that they always emphasized the importance of meaning instead of language and pursued connotations beyond literal forms; however, at the same time, the chinese language directly explores the meaning embedded in the forms of chinese charasters through calligraphy. In this way, they actually share a same goal through different means, directly pointing to the nature of the world.

> Ancient Chinese people believed that it is important to deliver intended meaning without being bound by wording, leave certain things unsaid, and generate meanings that could linger beyond rhyme. Such ideas seem to downplay the importance of the sounds and structures of written characters. However, through the art of calligraphy, they seek to directly <u>uncover</u> the significance inherent in their forms. In the end, both sounds and structures are enlisted to demonstrate the indescribable nature and meaning of the world.

## 12.5 HNNU MA 文学作品一言难尽

一个民族的文艺作品蕴涵着极其丰富的文化信息。这些信息根据不同的分类标准可分为显性文化和隐性文化，又可分成物质文化、精神文化、行为文化、制度文化等等，不一而足。文化信息不仅体现在作品的字面上，而且深藏于它的核心。读者通常在浏览作品时了解的往往是文本字面表达的文化信息，对其深层的文化信息可能不甚了了。但只要仔细、认真阅读都能领会其要旨，因为读者和作者同属一种文化。读者透过文字，凭借本文化的主要文化参数 (如原型、价值观等等)便能揣摩作者的意图。然而文化信息一经翻译，即从一种文化移植到另一种文化后，作品字面传递的文化信息与其深层文化及至整个文化体系之间的千丝万缕的联系被译者切断了，造成了“身首异处”的状态。这样，译语文化的读者在欣赏译本时只能看到译文字面提供的关于源语文化的信息。至于原著的深层文化信息，读者只能依据他所属的文化的主要参数加以释读。这不啻雾里看花，误读在所难免。面对文本中纷繁复杂的文化信息，译者不可能也不必把它们全部移植过去。

<u>1“.</u> <u>文艺作品”</u>是 literary and artistic works。单纯看这篇文章的话，其实指的就是 Literary works。 <u>2.“蕴含”</u>have/possess/contain/be home to。后面又出现了<u>“极其丰富的”</u>，那么也直接可以考虑简单的措辞，比 rich in。3.“浏览”browse。后面的“<u>文本字面</u>”，其实指的就是文字本身 words。<u>深层次的</u>，deeper/behind。4“. <u>不甚了了</u>”don’t know/have no idea/have no clue 5.“<u>仔细”=“认真</u>”carefully/in earnest。6.“<u>同属于一种文化</u>”belong to the same culture/be in the same cultural community/share the same cultural background。7.“<u>透过文字</u>”read。“<u>揣摩</u>”=“理解”，figure out/understand/make sense of。

8.看到“<u>一经翻译</u>”，立马想到了状语结构 once 引导。只不过，后面的“即”实际上是对翻译过程的一种解释，可能需要考虑同位语。“<u>千丝万缕的联系</u>”connections。“<u>切断</u>”cut off/sever。“<u>身首异处</u>”，实际上就是指的这种关系被切断了，造成了一种”“失联/断联” 的状态, disconnect。不翻译其实也可以，因为前面已经提到了“切断”。

9.“<u>释读</u>”=“理解”。“<u>不可能也不必</u>”cannot and shouldn’t。“<u>移植</u>”transplant/transfer

> A nation’s literary works are rich repositories of cultural information. By different standards, it can be classified as explicit and implicit culture, or material culture, spiritual culture, behavioral culture, and institutional culture. It is embedded not only in words but also in their deeper essence. When reading literary works, readers tend to grasp the cultural information conveyed by words and have little idea as to the <u>underlying</u> one. However, with careful reading, they can eventually make sense of it, particularly when they share the same cultural background with the authors. By relying on words and primary cultural parameters of their own society, including archetypes and values, readers can figure out the <u>intended</u> meaning in the end. But in the process of translation, the original information is transplanted into a different cultural context. It means that cultural information conveyed by words may be disconnected from the deeper cultural background and even the entire cultural system of the source language. As a result, readers from the target culture can only receive cultural information delivered by the translated words alone. As for the deeper layer of cultural information, it can only be inferred through the lens of readers’ own cultural parameters. This process is akin to viewing flowers in mist, leading to potential misunderstandings. Given the diversity and complexity of cultural information, translators can’t and shouldn’t transplant them all into another culture.

*be classified into + 名词/名词短语：“The books are classified into different subjects.” *be classified as：强调类别归属（即“被划分为……”）“This plant is classified as endangered.”

因为译者必须保持文艺作品的艺术性、可读性、美学功能等，他翻译作品主要是供广大读者欣赏而不是研究。如果把一部作品中的所有文化信息不论多少、不分主次逐一加注，那么有<u>关原著的文化信息</u>的注释在篇幅上可能会大大超过原著。这样译者奉献给读者的已经不是一部文艺作品，而是一部学术著作了。因此首先应该释读一部作品中的文化信息，然后将其分类，区分主次，有所取舍，实施传输。真可谓“有所为，有所不为”。

1.“<u>广大</u>”，没有什么实际含义，不译 2.“<u>有关原著的文化信息</u>”扔掉。3.“<u>奉献</u>”present 4.“<u>区分主次</u>”set priorities。“<u>有所取舍</u>”，简单来处理就是 make choices 5.“<u>实施传输</u>”，可以直译，那就是 convey them。也可以意译一下，把它理解成：动手开始翻译。或者再简单一点：动笔 put pen to paper 6.“<u>有所为，有所不为</u>”dos and don’ts

> That is because translators should preserve the artistry, readability and aesthetic functions of literary works, and their translations are meant to be read, rather than studied, by readers. In a book, if all the pieces of cultural information are annotated regardless of their quantity or significance, <u>then the annotations would far exceed the book itself in terms of length</u>. In the end, translators present readers not a literary book, but a scholarly treatise. That is why it is important to understand all the pieces of cultural information, classify them, and determine their significance before deciding to put pen to paper. This is the dos and don’ts of literary translation.

> The cultural and artistic works of a nation involve exceedingly rich cultural information. According to different standards of categorization, they can be divided into explicit and implicit culture, or material, spiritual, behavioral, institutional culture, etc. Cultural information is not only reflected on the literal forms of works, but also hide in the depths of its core. When looking through these works, readers often merely know the cultural information expressed by their literal surface, and may have little mastery of the deep-seated cultural information. However, they can apprehend the gist of a work through careful and scrupulous reading for that they and the author belong to the same culture. With major cultural indexes of their culture ( including original examples, and values), readers can speculate the author’s intentions beyond words. Once the information is translated into another language, however, namely it is grafted from the original culture into another culture, the ties between the cultural information and the culture behind it and even the entire cultural system are cut by the translator. As a result, the head and body of the work exist in different areas. Therefore, when reading translations, readers within the targeted culture could only acquire information concerning the original culture provided by the literal form of translations. As for the deep-level information embedded in the original works, they can only rely on the indexes of their own cultures to grasp it. Similar to watching flowers in the fog, it is inevitable to generate misunderstanding. Confronted with the complex cultural information within the text ,translators cannot and have not to translate all of them.

> Translators must ensure the artistic value, accessibility and aesthetic functions of cultural and artistic works because they translate works mainly for the readers at large. If they add notes for all the cultural information in a work one by one regardless of the number and importance of information, the length of notes will largely surpass the original work. In doing so, what translators contribute to the readers is not a cultural and artistic work but an academic work. Therefore, translators should firstly grasp the cultural information and then classify them according to the level of importance, and practice translation by discarding certain information. This is called "Do something worthy of being done."
